## Core Business Questions

- How many orders were recorded?
- How many units were sold?
- What is total revenue?
- Which product contributes the most revenue?
- Which channel performs best?
- Which region performs best?
- Is revenue increasing over time?
- Does sales volume broadly move with revenue?
- What should management pay attention to?

## KPI Presentation

- Orders represent transaction volume.
- Units represent quantity sold.
- Revenue represents monetary performance.
- KPI values should be shown before detailed charts.
- A KPI should have a clear unit.
- Revenue is expressed in lakh in this exercise.
- Avoid mixing units without labels.
- Use consistent periods for KPI comparisons.

## Monthly Analysis

- Group sales data by month.
- Compare orders across months.
- Compare units across months.
- Compare revenue across months.
- Look for sustained direction rather than one isolated point.
- A line chart is useful for time trends.
- Label the month axis clearly.
- Check the first and last period.
- Calculate growth when required.

## Product Analysis

- Rank products by revenue.
- Compare product orders.
- Compare units.
- Identify the leading contributor.
- A bar chart makes ranking easy to read.
- Do not confuse unit leadership with revenue leadership.
- Product mix can explain revenue differences.
- Use the same period for all products.

## Channel Analysis

- Website, App and Marketplace are compared.
- Revenue is a primary comparison metric.
- Orders and units add context.
- A channel leader can indicate where sales are concentrated.
- Channel performance should be interpreted with customer and cost data when available.
- Avoid claiming profitability from revenue alone.

## Regional Analysis

- Compare North, South, East and West.
- Revenue ranking identifies strong regions.
- Units provide volume context.
- Regional results may reflect product mix.
- A region with high units is not automatically highest in revenue.
- Use consistent metrics across regions.
- Present the ranking before explaining possible reasons.

## Presentation Structure

- Slide 1: title and objective.
- Slide 2: KPI summary.
- Slide 3: monthly revenue trend.
- Slide 4: product performance.
- Slide 5: channel performance.
- Slide 6: regional performance.
- Slide 7: key insights.
- Slide 8: recommended business focus.
- Keep each slide focused.

## Insight Writing

- Start with the observation.
- Add the metric.
- Explain the business meaning.
- Avoid unsupported causes.
- Example: revenue increased across the period.
- Example: one product leads revenue.
- Example: one channel leads channel revenue.
- Insights should be traceable to workbook data.

## Data Quality

- Check missing dates.
- Check missing product names.
- Check missing channel names.
- Check numeric fields.
- Check duplicate records.
- Check revenue units.
- Check period consistency.
- Validate totals before presenting.
- Never present unverified KPIs.

## Presentation Rules

- Use one message per chart.
- Use descriptive chart titles.
- Label axes.
- Show units.
- Keep comparisons consistent.
- Highlight the key observation verbally.
- Avoid overcrowding.
- Do not claim causation without evidence.
- End with actionable focus.

## Common Mistakes

- Presenting raw tables without interpretation.
- Using unlabeled revenue units.
- Mixing monthly and yearly periods.
- Ranking by the wrong metric.
- Confusing orders with units.
- Calling revenue profit.
- Claiming a reason not present in data.
- Ignoring data quality.
- Showing too many charts on one slide.
