# 📝 Day 056 Notes

## 1. Raw Material Inventory

Raw materials are inputs required for manufacturing or production activities.

Inventory analysis helps a business understand how much material is available and how material is being consumed.

## 2. Opening Inventory

Opening inventory represents the quantity of raw material available at the beginning of the period.

## 3. Purchases

Purchases represent material received during the period.

Purchasing decisions should consider expected consumption and replenishment requirements.

## 4. Material Usage

Material usage represents the quantity of raw material consumed during operations.

Usage is important for understanding demand on inventory.

## 5. Closing Inventory

Closing inventory represents the quantity remaining at the end of the period.

## 6. Inventory Flow

A simplified inventory relationship is:

Opening Inventory + Purchases − Material Usage = Closing Inventory

This relationship provides a basic check on inventory movement.

## 7. Inventory Value

Inventory value estimates the monetary value of available inventory.

Formula:

Closing Inventory × Unit Cost

## 8. Unit Cost

Unit cost represents the illustrative cost associated with one unit of raw material.

## 9. Lead Time

Lead time represents the number of days required to replenish material.

Longer lead times can increase the amount of inventory required for uninterrupted operations.

## 10. Average Daily Usage

Average daily usage represents the approximate amount of material consumed per day.

A simple calculation can be:

Material Usage ÷ Number of Days

## 11. Lead-Time Demand

Lead-time demand estimates expected usage during the replenishment period.

Formula:

Average Daily Usage × Lead Time

## 12. Safety Stock

Safety stock is additional inventory maintained as protection against uncertainty.

It can help provide additional coverage when demand or replenishment timing varies.

## 13. Reorder Point

Reorder point combines expected lead-time demand with a safety requirement.

Formula:

Lead-Time Demand + Safety Stock

## 14. Inventory Gap

Inventory gap compares closing inventory with reorder point.

Formula:

Closing Inventory − Reorder Point

## 15. Gap Interpretation

Negative gap:

Closing inventory is below the reorder point.

Zero gap:

Closing inventory equals the reorder point.

Positive gap:

Closing inventory is above the reorder point.

## 16. Stock Status

The workbook classifies records into:

- Below Reorder Point
- At/Above Reorder Point

## 18. Monthly Analysis

Monthly analysis helps identify changes over time.

The analysis includes:

## 19. Regional Analysis

Regional analysis compares:

- North
- South
- West

This can help identify differences in inventory conditions across locations.

## 20. Presentation

The purpose of presentation is to convert calculations into understandable business information.

A presentation should explain both the numbers and their business meaning.

## 21. Presentation Flow

A useful flow is:

Business Context

→ Data Review

→ Inventory Position

→ Usage and Purchases

→ Lead-Time Analysis

→ Reorder Analysis

→ Risk Signals

→ Business Insights

→ Actions
