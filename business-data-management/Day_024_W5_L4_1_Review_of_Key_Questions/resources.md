## 🧠 Business Analysis Resources

Business analysis starts with a question.

The question determines the required data.

The required data determines the calculation.

The calculation produces evidence.

Evidence supports the insight.

The insight should be relevant to the business question.

A number alone is not always an insight.

## 💰 Revenue Reference

Revenue represents sales value.

Total revenue represents the aggregate sales value.

Revenue can be grouped by time.

Revenue can be grouped by category.

Revenue can be grouped by channel.

Revenue can be grouped by region.

Revenue can be grouped by customer type.

Revenue is not automatically profit.

Cost data is required for profitability analysis.

## 📦 Volume Reference

Orders represent transaction activity.

Units represent product volume.

Orders and units can be analysed separately.

Revenue can be compared with orders.

Revenue can be compared with units.

Different measures can provide different perspectives.

Volume alone does not establish profitability.

## 🏷️ Pricing Reference

Discount percentage provides pricing context.

Average discount can be calculated.

Average discount can be compared across categories.

Discount should not be treated as profit.

A high discount does not automatically indicate poor performance.

A low discount does not automatically indicate high performance.

Margin data would provide stronger financial context.

## 👥 Customer Reference

Customer Type provides a grouping dimension.

New customers can be compared with Returning customers.

Revenue can be compared between customer groups.

Orders can be compared between customer groups.

Units can be compared between customer groups.

Customer acquisition data can provide more context.

Repeat purchase data can provide more context.

Customer lifetime value can provide deeper analysis.

## 🌍 Regional Reference

Region provides geographic grouping.

Regional revenue can identify strong observed markets.

Regional orders provide volume context.

Regional units provide additional context.

Detailed location data could improve analysis.

A single metric should not define complete regional performance.

## ⚠️ Interpretation Resources

Use descriptive language for observed results.

Avoid unsupported causal statements.

A revenue increase does not automatically identify its cause.

A high-performing category does not automatically have higher profit.

A high-discount category does not automatically perform poorly.

Additional variables may be required.

State limitations when appropriate.
