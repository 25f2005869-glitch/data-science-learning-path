## 1. Introduction

Business management is the process of planning, organising, leading, monitoring, reviewing, and improving business activities.

Management connects business goals with people, resources, processes, budgets, and performance measures.

A manager uses information about business performance to understand whether activities are progressing according to plan.

## 2. Management Cycle

A simple management cycle is:

**Plan → Organise → Lead → Monitor → Review → Improve**

Each stage supports the next stage.

## 3. Planning

Planning defines what the business wants to achieve and how it will use its resources.

Planning may include:

- Goals
- Targets
- Priorities
- Budgets
- Timelines
- Resources

A target provides a planned level of performance against which actual results can be compared.

## 4. Organising

Organising involves allocating resources and responsibilities.

Resources may include:

- Employees
- Money
- Technology
- Equipment
- Information
- Time

Managers need to coordinate resources so that business activities can be performed effectively.

## 5. Leading

Leading involves guiding and coordinating people.

Managers may:

- Communicate expectations.
- Assign responsibilities.
- Coordinate teams.
- Support employees.
- Resolve operational issues.
- Maintain focus on business goals.

## 6. Monitoring

Monitoring means regularly tracking business performance.

Managers can use KPIs to monitor:

- Actual performance
- Costs
- Budget utilisation
- Task completion
- Customer satisfaction
- Operational progress

Monitoring helps identify differences between planned and actual performance.

## 7. Reviewing Performance

Reviewing involves comparing results with the original plan.

For example:

**Target = ₹1,000,000**

**Actual = ₹950,000**

The manager can calculate target achievement and investigate the difference.

The comparison identifies a performance gap, but the KPI alone does not explain why the gap occurred.

## 8. Improving Business Activities

After reviewing performance, managers can identify areas for improvement.

Possible areas include:

- Resource allocation
- Processes
- Staffing
- Budget planning
- Task scheduling
- Customer service
- Communication

Improvement should be based on available evidence and business context.

## 9. Target vs Actual

Two important management measures are:

**Target:** Planned result.

**Actual:** Result achieved.

A simple comparison is:

**Target Achievement (%) = Actual / Target × 100**

For example:

Target = ₹500,000

Actual = ₹525,000

Target Achievement:

**525,000 / 500,000 × 100 = 105%**

## 10. Budget Management

A budget represents planned spending.

Actual cost represents the amount actually spent.

A simple cost variance is:

**Cost Variance = Actual Cost − Budget**

If actual cost is greater than the budget, the variance is positive.

If actual cost is lower than the budget, the variance is negative.

The reason for a variance requires further investigation.

## 11. Budget Utilisation

Budget utilisation measures how much of the allocated budget has been used.

**Budget Utilisation (%) = Actual Cost / Budget × 100**

For example:

Budget = ₹200,000

Actual Cost = ₹180,000

Budget Utilisation:

**180,000 / 200,000 × 100 = 90%**

## 12. Task Management

Managers can compare planned tasks with completed tasks.

**Task Completion (%) = Tasks Completed / Tasks Planned × 100**

For example:

Tasks Planned = 100

Tasks Completed = 90

Task Completion:

**90 / 100 × 100 = 90%**

This indicates the proportion of planned work completed.

## 13. Customer Satisfaction

Customer satisfaction is a customer-facing performance measure.

A manager can compare satisfaction levels across:

- Business functions
- Teams
- Months
- Processes

Customer satisfaction should be considered together with other operational measures.

## 14. Business Functions and Management

Different business functions may have different targets and responsibilities.

Examples include:

### Sales

Focuses on sales targets, orders, customers, and revenue.

### Operations

Focuses on delivering products or services and managing operational activities.

### Marketing

Focuses on demand generation, communication, and customer engagement.

### Customer Service

Focuses on customer requests, support, complaints, and satisfaction.

Management coordinates these functions so that their activities support broader business objectives.

## 15. Management KPIs

Important management KPIs include:

- Target Achievement
- Budget Utilisation
- Task Completion
- Customer Satisfaction
- Actual Cost
- Cost Variance
- Actual Performance

No single KPI gives a complete picture of business management.

Managers should consider several measures together.

## 16. Data-Driven Management

Business data helps managers understand performance.

A basic workflow is:

**Collect Data → Calculate KPIs → Compare Results → Investigate Gaps → Take Action → Review Again**

For example, if task completion decreases, a manager may investigate workload, staffing, deadlines, resources, or process issues.

The data provides evidence for investigation, but additional information may be needed to identify the cause.

## 17. Example

Suppose a business has a monthly target of ₹1,200,000 and achieves ₹1,140,000.

Target achievement is:

**1,140,000 / 1,200,000 × 100 = 95%**

The result is below the target.

A manager could then review:

- Sales activity
- Team capacity
- Customer demand
- Operational constraints
- Budget
- Process performance

The target achievement percentage identifies the difference from the plan; it does not by itself identify the cause.

## 18. Important Points

- Management connects business goals with execution.
- Planning establishes goals and targets.
- Organising allocates resources and responsibilities.
- Leading coordinates people.
- Monitoring tracks performance.
- Reviewing compares actual results with plans.
- Improvement focuses on future performance.
- Targets and actual results should be compared.
- Budget and actual cost should be reviewed together.
- Multiple KPIs provide broader business context.
- Data supports management decisions, but evidence should be interpreted carefully.

## 19. Summary

How businesses are managed can be understood through the cycle:

**Plan → Organise → Lead → Monitor → Review → Improve**

Managers use targets, budgets, tasks, costs, and customer measures to monitor business performance.

Business analytics helps convert management data into useful summaries and KPIs. These measures help identify performance gaps and areas that may require further investigation.
