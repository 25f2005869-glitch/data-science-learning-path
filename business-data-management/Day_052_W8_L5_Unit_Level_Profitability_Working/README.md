## 🎯 Learning Objective

Day 052 focuses on detailed **unit-level profitability working**.

The workflow connects sales volume, selling price, revenue, cost components, gross profit, unit cost, unit profit and margin.

The workbook uses an illustrative ACE Gears-style manufacturing dataset created for practice.

This is not official IIT Madras data and should not be interpreted as actual company performance.

## 📚 Learning Flow

1. Open the raw profitability data.
2. Understand sales units and selling price.
3. Calculate revenue.
4. Calculate material cost.
5. Calculate labour cost.
6. Calculate overhead cost.
7. Calculate total cost.
8. Calculate unit cost.
9. Calculate gross profit.
10. Calculate unit profit.
11. Calculate margin percentage.
12. Compare product profitability.
13. Review monthly profitability.
14. Review regional profitability.

## 🧮 Core Formulas

`Revenue = Sales Units × Selling Price per Unit`

`Material Cost = Sales Units × Material Cost per Unit`

`Labour Cost = Sales Units × Labour Cost per Unit`

`Overhead Cost = Sales Units × Overhead per Unit`

`Total Cost = Material Cost + Labour Cost + Overhead Cost`

`Unit Cost = Total Cost ÷ Sales Units`

`Gross Profit = Revenue − Total Cost`

`Unit Profit = Gross Profit ÷ Sales Units`

`Margin % = Gross Profit ÷ Revenue`

## 🧠 Business Interpretation

Unit-level analysis helps explain the economics of individual saleable units.

Total profit and unit profit answer different questions.

Revenue should be considered together with cost.

Margin provides a relative profitability measure.

## 🔎 Review Checklist

- Check the input values before calculating.
- Trace the formula from units to revenue.
- Trace each cost component separately.
- Calculate total cost before profit.
- Convert total cost into unit cost.
- Calculate gross profit from revenue and total cost.
- Calculate unit profit from gross profit and units.
- Calculate margin as a percentage of revenue.
- Compare products using the same metric.
- Compare months using the same metric.
- Review regional differences carefully.
- Use the dashboard as a visual summary.
