# 📝 Day 043 Notes — Revenue Analysis Presentation

**Author:** Saloni Tiwari  
**Programme:** IIT Madras BS Degree — Diploma Level  
**Day:** 043  
**Topic:** W7_L6 Revenue Analysis Presentation

## 1. Presentation Purpose
Analysis becomes useful when results can be communicated clearly.
A revenue presentation should answer a defined business question.

## 2. Start With the Question
Do not begin with charts.
Begin by defining what the audience needs to understand.

## 3. Executive KPIs
Useful headline measures include:
- Total Revenue
- Highest Revenue Month
- Top Product
- Top Region

## 4. Monthly Revenue
Monthly revenue shows movement over time.
A line chart is suitable for communicating this pattern.

## 5. MoM Growth
MoM Growth = (Current Revenue - Previous Revenue) / Previous Revenue.
It provides a percentage view of month-to-month movement.

## 6. Product Analysis
Product revenue identifies contribution by product.
Ranking makes the comparison easier to communicate.

## 7. Regional Analysis
Regional revenue identifies geographical contribution.
It provides a supporting dimension for the overall revenue story.

## 8. Gross Margin
Gross Margin = Revenue - Production Cost.
Revenue should not be treated as the only financial measure.

## 9. Revenue Share
Revenue Share = Segment Revenue / Total Revenue.
It describes contribution to the overall total.

## 10. Evidence-Based Story
A presentation can contain:
- Observation
- Calculation
- Interpretation
- Hypothesis

## 11. Observation
An observation directly describes a value or pattern in the data.

## 12. Hypothesis
A hypothesis is a possible explanation.
It requires additional evidence before being treated as a confirmed cause.

## 13. Slide Sequence
1. Title
2. Business Question
3. Executive KPI
4. Monthly Trend
5. Product Analysis
6. Regional Analysis
7. Margin Context
8. Key Insights
9. Open Questions

## 14. Chart Selection
Line chart → time trend.
Column chart → category comparison.

## 15. Chart Titles
Use titles that state the metric and dimension.
Avoid vague titles.

## 16. Axes
Label important axes.
Include units where necessary.

## 17. Presentation Narrative
Move from broad to detailed.
Start with the overall result.
Then show supporting breakdowns.

## 18. Product Contribution
Product contribution explains how the total is distributed across products.
Contribution is not the same as profitability.

## 19. Regional Contribution
Regional contribution explains how the total is distributed geographically.
It does not by itself explain causes.

## 20. Margin Context
Revenue and gross margin provide different financial information.
Comparing both gives additional context.

## 21. Business Questions
Ask:
- What changed?
- Where did it change?
- How large was the change?
- What evidence could explain it?

## 22. Dashboard
The dashboard combines headline KPIs and supporting charts.
It is useful for presentation practice.

## 23. Data Quality
Check missing values.
Check duplicate records.
Check chronological ordering.
Check numerical fields.

## 24. Practical Workflow
Raw Data → Summary → KPI → Trend → Breakdown → Interpretation → Presentation.

## 25. Dataset Limitation
The workbook is illustrative.
It does not reproduce proprietary course data.

## 26. Key Takeaway
A good revenue presentation is concise, evidence-based, and structured around a business question.
