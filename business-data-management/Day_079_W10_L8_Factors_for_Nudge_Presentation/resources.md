## 6. Presentation Learning
- Begin with a clear business question.
- Define the population.
- Define the factor.
- Define the outcome.
- State the denominator.
- Show the baseline.
- Show the comparison.
- Explain limitations.

## 7. Experimental Thinking
- Random assignment can improve causal inference.
- Treatment and control groups should be clearly defined.
- The outcome metric should be specified before comparison.
- Sample size matters for statistical reliability.
- Exposure imbalance can affect interpretation.

## 8. Confounding
- A confounder is associated with both the factor and outcome.
- Device may vary across customer segments.
- Category may vary across nudge types.
- Region composition can differ across groups.
- Observational differences can therefore have multiple explanations.

## 9. Statistical Extensions
- Mean comparison.
- Proportion comparison.
- Confidence intervals.
- Hypothesis testing.
- Chi-square tests for categorical outcomes.
- Logistic regression for binary outcomes.
- A/B testing.

## 10. Dashboard Practice

- Keep titles question-oriented.

## 12. Evidence Standards
- Separate observation from explanation.
- Separate association from causality.
- State synthetic-data limitations.
- Avoid generalising beyond the dataset.
- Document assumptions.
