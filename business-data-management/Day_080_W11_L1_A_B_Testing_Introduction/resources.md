## 7. Causal Inference
- Random assignment reduces systematic pre-treatment differences in expectation.
- Control provides the counterfactual comparison within the experiment.
- Proper experimental design is more important than simply comparing percentages.
- Confounding is a central concern in non-randomised comparisons.

## 8. Sample Size
- Always report counts with rates.
- Small groups can have unstable estimates.
- Power depends on effect size, sample size and variability.
- Larger samples generally improve precision.

## 9. Practical Significance
- A statistically detectable difference may be operationally small.
- A practically meaningful difference may require adequate sample size.
- Business impact should consider cost and benefit.
- Conversion alone may not capture the complete business outcome.

## 10. Dashboard Resources
- Use a clear control/treatment comparison.
- Show exact counts near rates when possible.
- Keep chart titles tied to business questions.
- Avoid implying causality through chart titles.

## 13. Advanced Extension
- Logistic regression can model binary outcomes.
- Interaction terms can examine heterogeneous treatment effects.

## 14. Evidence Discipline
- State exactly what was measured.
- State the denominator.
- Separate descriptive results from causal claims.
- Document assumptions.
- Label synthetic datasets clearly.
