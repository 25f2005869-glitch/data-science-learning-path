## 1. Introduction

Day 001 is the introductory session of the **Business Data Management (BDM)** course.

The purpose of this session is to understand the overall direction of the course and how business concepts, data, spreadsheets, analysis, and decision-making are connected.

The course follows a practical learning approach in which business situations are studied using data and analytical tools.

---

## 2. What is Business Data Management?

Business Data Management is concerned with understanding and working with data generated through business activities.

Businesses generate data through different processes such as:

- Sales
- Customers
- Finance
- Inventory
- Human Resources
- Manufacturing
- Marketing
- Payments
- Operations

Business data can be analysed to understand what is happening in a business and to support better decisions.

---

## 3. Business and Data

A business performs many activities every day.

These activities generate information that can be represented as data.

For example:

- A sale generates sales data.
- A customer interaction generates customer data.
- An employee recruitment process generates HR data.
- A manufacturing process generates production data.
- An inventory process generates stock data.
- A financial transaction generates financial data.

Therefore, business activities and data are closely connected.

---

## 4. Importance of Business Data

Business data helps organisations understand their activities and performance.

Data can be used to:

- Measure performance
- Identify patterns
- Compare results
- Understand customers
- Analyse sales
- Study costs
- Monitor inventory
- Evaluate business performance
- Support decision-making

The usefulness of data depends not only on collecting it but also on correctly organising and analysing it.

---

## 5. From Data to Decision

A basic business analytics process can be represented as:

Business Activity → Data → Analysis → Insight → Decision

### Business Activity

A business performs an activity such as selling a product.

### Data

The activity generates information such as:

- Product
- Quantity
- Price
- Date
- Customer
- Revenue

### Analysis

The collected data can be examined to identify patterns and relationships.

### Insight

Analysis can reveal useful information, such as changes in sales or differences between products.

### Decision

The business can use the insight to make an informed decision.

## 6. Role of Spreadsheets

Spreadsheets are an important tool in Business Data Management.

The course introduces the use of spreadsheets for working with business data.

Spreadsheets can be used for:

- Data entry
- Data organisation
- Calculations
- Formulas
- Functions
- Data summarisation
- Data analysis
- Tables
- Charts
- Business reporting

The BDM learning journey includes practical spreadsheet-based analysis.

## 7. Practical Learning Approach

The BDM course is designed around practical business situations and case studies.

Instead of studying business concepts in isolation, the learning process connects concepts with data and analysis.

A simplified approach is:

Business Problem
↓
Understand the Problem
↓
Identify the Data
↓
Organise the Data
↓
Analyse the Data
↓
Generate Insights
↓
Support Business Decisions

This approach will become increasingly important in the later case studies.

## 9. Case-Study-Based Learning

A major part of the BDM learning approach involves business case studies.

Case studies provide a realistic business context in which data can be analysed.

The general process is:

1. Understand the business situation.
2. Identify the business questions.
3. Examine the available data.
4. Analyse the data.
5. Present the findings.
6. Derive useful business insights.

This helps develop both technical and business understanding.

---

## 10. Data Analysis and Business Understanding

Data analysis alone is not enough.

A good business data analyst should understand:

- What the business problem is
- What the data represents
- Which analysis is appropriate
- What the results mean
- How the results can support a business decision

Therefore, BDM combines **business understanding** with **data analysis**.

## 12. Key Takeaways

- Business activities generate data.
- Business data can be organised and analysed.
- Data analysis can produce useful business insights.
- Business insights can support decision-making.
- Spreadsheets are an important practical tool for BDM.
- BDM follows a practical and case-study-oriented learning approach.
- Understanding the business problem is important before analysing data.
- Data analysis should ultimately connect back to the business objective.
