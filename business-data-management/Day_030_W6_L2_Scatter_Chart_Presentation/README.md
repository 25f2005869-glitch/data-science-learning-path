## Scatter Chart Concept

- A scatter chart plots individual observations as points.
- Each point represents one observation.
- The X-axis contains one numeric variable.
- The Y-axis contains another numeric variable.
- The position of a point depends on both variables.
- A pattern can reveal a relationship.
- A scattered cloud can indicate a weak relationship.
- A directional pattern can indicate a positive or negative relationship.

## Presentation Flow

- Start with the business question.
- Introduce the X-axis.
- Introduce the Y-axis.
- Show the scatter chart.
- Describe the visible pattern.
- Mention correlation if calculated.
- Discuss unusual observations.
- State the business implication carefully.
- Avoid unsupported causal claims.

## Business Question

- A useful question is: Does higher ad spend broadly align with higher revenue?
- The scatter chart can visually support this question.
- The relationship should be assessed across observations.
- Product and channel can be used as context.
- Revenue should not automatically be interpreted as profit.

## Interpretation

- An upward pattern indicates positive association.
- A downward pattern indicates negative association.
- A random cloud indicates little visible linear association.
- Points far from the general pattern may be unusual observations.
- Correlation measures linear association.
- Correlation alone does not establish causation.

## Common Mistakes

- Using a line chart for unrelated observations.
- Putting a categorical variable on the numeric axis.
- Forgetting axis units.
- Assuming correlation means causation.
- Ignoring outliers.
- Reading only one point.
- Claiming profitability from revenue.
- Presenting a chart without a business question.
