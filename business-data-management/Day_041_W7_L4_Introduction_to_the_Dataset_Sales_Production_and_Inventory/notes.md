# 📝 Day 041 Notes — Introduction to the Dataset

**Author:** Saloni Tiwari  
**Programme:** IIT Madras BS Degree — Diploma Level  
**Day:** 041  
**Topic:** W7_L4 Introduction to the Dataset — Sales, Production and Inventory

## 1. Dataset Understanding

Before analysis, understand what each row represents.

A dataset is a structured collection of observations.

Each observation contains values for several variables.

## 2. Business Dataset

This practical uses a manufacturing business dataset.

The dataset connects operational and financial information.

The major areas are:

- Sales
- Production
- Inventory
- Revenue
- Cost

## 3. Product

Product identifies the manufactured item.

The illustrative dataset contains:

- Gears
- Shafts
- Bearings

## 4. Region

Region identifies the geographical business area.

The illustrative dataset contains:

- North
- South
- West

## 5. Customer Type

Customer type identifies the broad customer segment.

Examples include:

- OEM
- Distributor

## 6. Production Units

Production Units represent the number of units produced.

Production information helps understand manufacturing capacity and activity.

## 7. Sales Units

Sales Units represent the number of units sold.

Sales units are an important operational sales measure.

## 8. Scrap Units

Scrap Units represent units classified as scrap.

Scrap can be studied against production.

## 9. Closing Inventory

Closing Inventory represents inventory remaining at the end of the observation period.

It connects production and sales with stock management.

## 10. Revenue

Revenue represents the sales value generated.

Revenue is a major financial measure.

## 11. Production Cost

Production Cost represents the cost associated with production.

It can later be compared with revenue.

## 12. Dataset Dimensions

Dimensions describe categories.

Examples:

- Product
- Region
- Customer Type
- Month

## 13. Dataset Measures

Measures contain numerical business values.

Examples:

- Production Units
- Sales Units
- Scrap Units
- Closing Inventory
- Revenue
- Production Cost

## 14. Sales Analysis

Sales analysis can answer:

- How many units were sold?
- How much revenue was generated?
- Which product generated more revenue?
- Which region generated more revenue?

## 15. Production Analysis

Production analysis can answer:

- How many units were produced?
- How many units became scrap?
- Which product had higher production?

## 16. Inventory Analysis

Inventory analysis can answer:

- How much stock remains?
- Which product has higher inventory?
- How does inventory change over time?

## 17. Monthly Analysis

Monthly grouping converts detailed observations into time-based summaries.

This is useful for trend analysis.

## 18. Data Dictionary

A Data Dictionary describes:

- Field name
- Meaning
- Business interpretation

It should be reviewed before calculations.

## 19. Data Quality

Important checks include:

- Missing values
- Duplicate records
- Incorrect data types
- Invalid categories
- Unexpected numerical values

## 20. Business Interpretation

Numbers should be connected to business questions.

Do not calculate metrics without knowing what decision they support.

## 21. Sales and Production

Comparing sales units with production units can provide operational context.

A difference between production and sales can affect inventory.

## 22. Inventory Connection

A simplified relationship is:

Production + Opening Inventory - Sales = Closing Inventory

Actual inventory accounting can involve additional movements.

## 23. Revenue and Cost

Revenue and production cost can later be compared.

A simple gross margin concept is:

Revenue - Production Cost

## 24. Visualization

Charts help communicate patterns.

The dashboard contains:

- Sales vs Production
- Closing Inventory
- Monthly Revenue

## 25. Learning Sequence

Understand dataset first.

Then create summaries.

Then analyse trends.

Then communicate insights.

## 26. Practical Approach

Start from the raw data.

Do not immediately jump to the dashboard.

Understand the underlying records first.

## 27. Key Takeaway

Dataset understanding is the foundation of reliable business analysis.

A good analyst knows what each column means before using it.
