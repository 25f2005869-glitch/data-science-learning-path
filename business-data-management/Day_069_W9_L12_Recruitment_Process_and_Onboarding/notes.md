# 📝 Day 069 Notes — Recruitment Process and Onboarding

## 1. Recruitment Process

Recruitment is a sequence of activities used to move from a workforce requirement toward a selected candidate.

## 2. Requirement Identification

The process starts by defining why a position is required.

Headcount, role and business need should be documented.

## 3. Job Definition

A role should have a clear title, responsibilities, qualifications and required skills.

## 4. Sourcing

Sourcing identifies channels through which potential candidates can enter the process.

## 5. Recruitment Channels

Channels can include job portals, referrals, internal transfers and agencies.

## 6. Application Volume

Application count measures the volume entering the recruitment pipeline.

## 7. Screening

Screening checks whether applicants meet documented requirements.

## 8. Assessment

Assessment creates a structured numerical or categorical evaluation.

## 9. Interview

Interview information can be stored as a separate stage or assessment dimension.

## 10. Selection

Selection records the recruitment outcome.

## 11. Selected Flag

Selected_Flag uses 1 for selected and 0 for not selected in this practice dataset.

## 12. Selection Rate

Selection rate measures selected candidates relative to the candidate records being analysed.

## 13. Days to Fill

Days_to_Fill is used as a process-duration metric.

## 14. Recruitment Cost

Recruitment cost represents the illustrative cost associated with a recruitment record.

## 15. Cost Per Day

Cost per day divides recruitment cost by days to fill.

## 16. Cost Per Selected

Cost per selected candidate divides total recruitment cost by selected candidates.

## 17. Channel Analysis

Grouping by recruitment channel allows volume, outcome, time and cost to be compared.

## 18. Department Analysis

Department grouping shows how recruitment activity differs across functions.

## 19. Monthly Analysis

Monthly grouping helps identify changes in recruitment activity over time.

## 20. Stage Analysis

Stage counts show the distribution of records across recruitment stages.

## 21. Process Monitoring

A process dashboard can show volume, duration and outcome together.

## 22. Onboarding

Onboarding begins after the selection and offer stages and prepares a new employee for productive participation.

## 23. Pre-Joining

Pre-joining tasks may include documentation and background verification.

## 24. Joining

Joining activities may include orientation, manager introduction and access setup.

## 25. Task Status

Task status distinguishes completed work from work still in progress.

## 26. Access Readiness

Access readiness indicates whether required organisational or technology access is available.

## 27. Onboarding Duration

Task_Days provides an illustrative measure of task duration.

## 28. Completion Rate

Completion rate = completed tasks / total tasks × 100.

## 29. Recruitment vs Onboarding

Recruitment and onboarding should be analysed as connected but separate processes.

## 30. Data Quality

Candidate IDs should be checked for duplicates.

## 31. Numeric Validation

Days, costs, applications and scores should be numeric.

## 32. Category Validation

Department, channel, stage and role values should use consistent labels.

## 33. Date Validation

Application and stage dates should be valid and logically ordered.

## 34. Cost Interpretation

A low recruitment cost does not automatically imply a better process.

## 35. Time Interpretation

A shorter process should be considered alongside process outcome and role requirements.

## 36. Channel Interpretation

Channel performance should be examined using multiple measures rather than one KPI.

## 37. Dashboard Design

Each chart should answer a defined business question.

## 38. Synthetic Dataset

This workbook uses illustrative data for learning.

## 39. Business Use

A real HR analytics system can combine recruitment, employee and onboarding data.

## 40. Governance

Access to candidate information should be controlled in real-world systems.

## 41. Final Learning

Recruitment analytics measures how candidates move through a process.

## 42. Final Learning

Onboarding analytics measures whether selected candidates complete required transition tasks.

## 43. Analytical Connection

Recruitment creates the selected-candidate population.

Onboarding tracks the next operational stage.

## 44. Practical Skill

The key skill is translating a process into measurable fields and KPIs.

## 45. Review

Always inspect both the raw data and the aggregated summaries before drawing conclusions.

## 46. Recruitment Handoff

The handoff begins when a candidate moves from selection toward joining.

## 47. Offer Stage

Offer information should be recorded before onboarding tasks are assigned.

## 48. Joining Readiness

Joining readiness depends on completion of required pre-joining activities.

## 49. Task Ownership

Each onboarding task should have an owner in a real system.

## 50. SLA Thinking

A task can be monitored against a planned completion time.

## 51. Exception Tracking

Incomplete tasks should be visible for follow-up.

## 52. Process Bottlenecks

A repeated delay at one stage may indicate a process bottleneck.

## 53. Recruitment Funnel

The funnel can be represented as counts at successive stages.

## 54. Conversion

Stage-to-stage conversion can be calculated when reliable stage counts are available.

## 55. Candidate Experience

Recruitment process data can help identify unnecessary delays or repeated steps.

## 56. Audit Trail

Important process events should have timestamps in a production system.

## 57. Joining Date

A real onboarding model should include the planned and actual joining dates.

## 58. Training

Training completion can be added as a later onboarding metric.

## 59. Early Productivity

A mature onboarding dataset may eventually track time-to-productivity.

## 60. Final Review

The recruitment process describes movement toward selection.

Onboarding describes the transition from selected candidate to organisational readiness.
