## 🎯 1. Introduction

Demand and Supply are fundamental concepts in economics.

They help us understand:

- How consumers behave in a market
- How producers make decisions
- How prices are determined
- How much of a product is bought and sold
- Why shortages and surpluses occur
- How changes in market conditions affect businesses

In Business Data Management, Demand and Supply provide a foundation for understanding **market behavior, pricing, sales, market analysis, and business decision-making**.

---

## 📌 2. What is Demand?

**Demand** refers to the willingness and ability of consumers to purchase a good or service at different prices.

Demand is more than simply wanting a product.

For demand to exist, a consumer generally needs:

1. Desire to purchase
2. Ability to pay
3. Willingness to purchase at a particular price

### Example

Suppose a person wants to buy a laptop but does not have enough money to purchase it.

The person may have a desire for the laptop, but this does not necessarily represent effective demand.

---

## 📊 3. Quantity Demanded

**Quantity demanded** is the specific quantity of a good or service that consumers are willing and able to purchase at a particular price.

### Example

| Price | Quantity Demanded |
|---:|---:|
| ₹100 | 100 units |
| ₹120 | 80 units |
| ₹140 | 60 units |
| ₹160 | 40 units |
| ₹180 | 20 units |

As the price changes, the quantity demanded generally changes.

---

## 📉 4. Law of Demand

The **Law of Demand** describes the general inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded.

When other factors remain constant:

**Price ↑ → Quantity Demanded ↓**

**Price ↓ → Quantity Demanded ↑**

Therefore, price and quantity demanded generally move in **opposite directions**.

---

## 📈 5. Demand Curve

A **Demand Curve** represents the relationship between:

- Price
- Quantity Demanded

Usually:

- Price is shown on the **vertical axis**
- Quantity demanded is shown on the **horizontal axis**

A typical demand curve slopes downward from left to right.

### Basic Representation

Price  
↓  
Demand Curve slopes downward  
↓  
Quantity Demanded

The downward slope represents the inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded.

---

## 🔍 6. Factors Affecting Demand

Demand can be affected by several factors.

### 6.1 Price

The price of a product affects its quantity demanded.

Generally:

**Price ↑ → Quantity Demanded ↓**

**Price ↓ → Quantity Demanded ↑**

---

### 6.2 Income

Changes in consumer income can affect demand.

For many normal goods:

**Income ↑ → Demand ↑**

However, the effect depends on the type of good.

---

### 6.3 Consumer Preferences

Changes in consumer tastes, preferences, trends, and habits can affect demand.

### Example

If consumers become more interested in electric vehicles, demand for electric vehicles may increase.

---

### 6.4 Prices of Related Goods

The prices of related products can affect demand.

Related goods include:

- Substitutes
- Complements

### Substitutes

Substitutes are products that can serve similar purposes.

Example:

**Tea ↔ Coffee**

If the price of coffee increases, some consumers may shift toward tea.

### Complements

Complements are goods that are commonly used together.

Example:

**Car ↔ Petrol**

A change in the price of one may affect demand for the other.

---

### 6.5 Expectations

Consumer expectations about future prices, income, or market conditions can affect current demand.

For example, if consumers expect prices to increase in the future, they may purchase more today.

---

### 6.6 Number of Consumers

An increase in the number of potential consumers can increase market demand.

---

## 📌 7. Demand vs Quantity Demanded

These two concepts should not be confused.

| Demand | Quantity Demanded |
|---|---|
| Overall relationship between price and quantity | Specific quantity at a particular price |
| Can be represented by a demand curve | Represents a particular point on the curve |
| Can change due to factors other than the product's own price | Changes due to a change in the product's own price, with other factors held constant |

---

## 📌 8. What is Supply?

**Supply** refers to the willingness and ability of producers to offer goods or services for sale at different prices.

Producers consider factors such as:

- Selling price
- Production costs
- Technology
- Availability of resources
- Expected future prices
- Number of sellers

---

## 📊 9. Quantity Supplied

**Quantity supplied** is the specific quantity of a good or service that producers are willing and able to sell at a particular price.

### Example

| Price | Quantity Supplied |
|---:|---:|
| ₹100 | 20 units |
| ₹120 | 40 units |
| ₹140 | 60 units |
| ₹160 | 80 units |
| ₹180 | 100 units |

---

## 📈 10. Law of Supply

The **Law of Supply** describes the general positive relationship between price and quantity supplied.

When other factors remain constant:

**Price ↑ → Quantity Supplied ↑**

**Price ↓ → Quantity Supplied ↓**

Therefore, price and quantity supplied generally move in the **same direction**.

---

## 📈 11. Supply Curve

A **Supply Curve** represents the relationship between:

- Price
- Quantity Supplied

Usually:

- Price is shown on the **vertical axis**
- Quantity supplied is shown on the **horizontal axis**

A typical supply curve slopes upward from left to right.

The upward slope represents the direct relationship between price and quantity supplied.

---

## 🔍 12. Factors Affecting Supply

Supply can be affected by several factors.

### 12.1 Price

A higher selling price generally provides producers with greater incentive to supply.

---

### 12.2 Cost of Production

If production costs increase, producers may reduce supply.

Example:

**Raw Material Cost ↑ → Production Cost ↑ → Supply may ↓**

---

### 12.3 Technology

Improved technology can make production more efficient.

Better technology can:

- Increase productivity
- Reduce production costs
- Increase potential supply

---

### 12.4 Availability of Resources

Availability of resources such as:

- Raw materials
- Labour
- Capital
- Equipment

can affect supply.

---

### 12.5 Expectations

Producer expectations about future prices and market conditions can affect current supply decisions.

---

### 12.6 Number of Sellers

An increase in the number of sellers can increase market supply.

---

## 📌 13. Supply vs Quantity Supplied

| Supply | Quantity Supplied |
|---|---|
| Overall relationship between price and quantity supplied | Specific quantity at a particular price |
| Represented by a supply curve | Represents a particular point on the curve |
| Can change because of factors such as technology and input costs | Changes with the product's own price, holding other factors constant |

---

## ⚖️ 14. Market Equilibrium

**Market Equilibrium** occurs when:

**Quantity Demanded = Quantity Supplied**

The price at which this happens is called the:

### Equilibrium Price

The quantity at this point is called the:

### Equilibrium Quantity

### Example

| Price | Quantity Demanded | Quantity Supplied |
|---:|---:|---:|
| ₹100 | 100 | 40 |
| ₹120 | 80 | 60 |
| ₹140 | 60 | 60 |
| ₹160 | 40 | 80 |
| ₹180 | 20 | 100 |

At **₹140**:

**Quantity Demanded = 60 units**

**Quantity Supplied = 60 units**

Therefore:

**Equilibrium Price = ₹140**

**Equilibrium Quantity = 60 units**

---

## 📉 15. Shortage / Excess Demand

A **shortage** occurs when:

**Quantity Demanded > Quantity Supplied**

### Example

Demand = 100 units

Supply = 60 units

Therefore:

**Shortage = 100 − 60 = 40 units**

A shortage means consumers want to purchase more than producers are supplying at that price.

---

## 📈 16. Surplus / Excess Supply

A **surplus** occurs when:

**Quantity Supplied > Quantity Demanded**

### Example

Supply = 100 units

Demand = 60 units

Therefore:

**Surplus = 100 − 60 = 40 units**

A surplus means producers are supplying more than consumers are willing to purchase at that price.

---

## 🔄 17. Movement Along a Curve

A change in the product's own price generally causes movement along an existing demand or supply curve.

### Demand

**Price changes → Quantity Demanded changes**

### Supply

**Price changes → Quantity Supplied changes**

The entire curve does not necessarily shift because of a change in the product's own price.

---

## 🔄 18. Shift of a Curve

A change in factors other than the product's own price can shift the entire demand or supply curve.

### Demand Curve Can Shift Due To:

- Income
- Consumer preferences
- Population
- Expectations
- Prices of related goods

### Supply Curve Can Shift Due To:

- Input costs
- Technology
- Number of sellers
- Resource availability
- Producer expectations

---

## 📊 19. Increase in Demand

An increase in demand means consumers want to purchase more at given prices.

Possible causes include:

- Higher income
- Increase in number of consumers
- Favorable consumer preferences
- Increase in price of substitutes
- Decrease in price of complements

An increase in demand is represented by a **rightward shift** of the demand curve.

---

## 📊 20. Decrease in Demand

A decrease in demand means consumers want to purchase less at given prices.

Possible causes include:

- Lower income
- Decrease in number of consumers
- Unfavorable preferences
- Decrease in price of substitutes
- Increase in price of complements

A decrease in demand is represented by a **leftward shift** of the demand curve.

---

## 📈 21. Increase in Supply

An increase in supply means producers are willing and able to supply more at given prices.

Possible causes include:

- Improved technology
- Lower production costs
- Better availability of resources
- Increase in number of sellers

An increase in supply is represented by a **rightward shift** of the supply curve.

---

## 📉 22. Decrease in Supply

A decrease in supply means producers are willing and able to supply less at given prices.

Possible causes include:

- Higher production costs
- Reduced availability of resources
- Production disruptions
- Reduction in number of sellers

A decrease in supply is represented by a **leftward shift** of the supply curve.

---

## 💼 23. Demand & Supply in Business

Demand and Supply concepts are important for business decisions.

### 💰 Pricing

Businesses need to understand how customers may respond to changes in price.

### 🏭 Production Planning

Businesses need to estimate how much should be produced.

### 📦 Inventory Planning

Businesses need to balance available inventory with expected demand.

### 📈 Sales Forecasting

Demand information can help businesses estimate potential sales.

### 📊 Market Analysis

Demand and Supply help businesses understand market conditions.

### 🎯 Resource Allocation

Businesses can use market information to decide where to allocate limited resources.

## 📊 24. Demand & Supply in Data Analysis

Demand and Supply concepts can be represented using business data.

### Example

| Month | Price | Demand | Supply |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| January | ₹100 | 1,000 | 700 |
| February | ₹110 | 900 | 800 |
| March | ₹120 | 800 | 800 |
| April | ₹130 | 700 | 900 |

A business analyst can analyze:

- Price trends
- Demand trends
- Supply trends
- Equilibrium
- Shortages
- Surpluses
- Market opportunities

This connects economic concepts with **Business Data Management and data-driven decision-making**.

---

## 🏪 25. Business Example

Suppose a company sells smartphones.

If the price of a smartphone decreases from ₹30,000 to ₹25,000, more consumers may be willing to purchase it.

This may increase the **quantity demanded**.

Now suppose the cost of producing the smartphone increases.

The producer may reduce the quantity supplied.

Therefore, the business needs to consider both sides:

**Consumer Side → Demand**

**Producer Side → Supply**

Understanding both sides can support better decisions about:

- Pricing
- Production
- Inventory
- Sales
- Market opportunities

## 🧠 26. Important Relationships

### Demand

**Price ↑ → Quantity Demanded ↓**

**Price ↓ → Quantity Demanded ↑**

### Supply

**Price ↑ → Quantity Supplied ↑**

**Price ↓ → Quantity Supplied ↓**

### Equilibrium

**Quantity Demanded = Quantity Supplied**

### Shortage

**Quantity Demanded > Quantity Supplied**

### Surplus

**Quantity Supplied > Quantity Demanded**

---

## 📋 27. Quick Comparison

| Concept | Relationship |
|---|---|
| Demand & Price | Inverse |
| Supply & Price | Direct |
| Equilibrium | Demand = Supply |
| Shortage | Demand > Supply |
| Surplus | Supply > Demand |
| Demand Increase | Demand curve shifts right |
| Demand Decrease | Demand curve shifts left |
| Supply Increase | Supply curve shifts right |
| Supply Decrease | Supply curve shifts left |

## 🎯 28. Key Takeaways

- Demand represents the consumer side of a market.
- Supply represents the producer side.
- Demand generally has an inverse relationship with price.
- Supply generally has a direct relationship with price.
- Demand and quantity demanded are different concepts.
- Supply and quantity supplied are different concepts.
- Equilibrium occurs when quantity demanded equals quantity supplied.
- A shortage occurs when quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied.
- A surplus occurs when quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded.
- A change in the product's own price generally causes movement along a curve.
- Changes in other relevant factors can shift the demand or supply curve.
- Demand and Supply are important for business analysis and decision-making.
