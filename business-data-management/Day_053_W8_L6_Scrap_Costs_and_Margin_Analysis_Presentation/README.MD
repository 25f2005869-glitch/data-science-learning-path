## 📌 Overview

Day 053 focuses on understanding scrap costs and their effect on manufacturing profitability.

In manufacturing, scrap refers to material or products that cannot be used or sold as intended because of defects, damage, production errors, or other process-related issues.

Scrap creates additional costs for a business. Analysing scrap alongside revenue, production cost, gross profit, and profit margin helps identify potential operational inefficiencies.

This practical exercise uses an illustrative manufacturing dataset to calculate scrap-related metrics, compare product performance, and prepare a dashboard for business presentation.

**Note:** The workbook uses illustrative educational data. It is not an official IIT Madras dataset or verified company data.

## 🧮 Important Formulas

### Scrap Rate

Scrap Rate (%) = Scrap Units / Production Units × 100

This measures the percentage of production units recorded as scrap.

### Unit Production Cost

Unit Production Cost = Material Cost per Unit + Labour Cost per Unit + Overhead Cost per Unit

This estimates the production cost associated with one unit.

### Scrap Cost

Scrap Cost = Scrap Units × Unit Production Cost

This estimates the production cost associated with the recorded scrap units.

### Revenue

Revenue = Sales Units × Selling Price per Unit

This calculates revenue from the units sold.

### Gross Profit

Gross Profit = Revenue − Cost of Sales

The result depends on how cost of sales is defined in the dataset.

### Gross Margin

Gross Margin (%) = Gross Profit / Revenue × 100

This measures gross profit as a percentage of revenue.

### Margin After Total Production Cost

Margin After Total Production Cost (%) = (Revenue − Total Production Cost) / Revenue × 100

This is a simplified comparison of revenue against total production cost. It should not automatically be interpreted as a standard accounting gross margin.

## 💡 Business Interpretation

Scrap analysis is useful because material and production resources are consumed even when units cannot be sold as intended.

However, a high scrap rate alone does not establish the reason for a production problem.

A complete investigation may also require information about:

- Product complexity
- Production volume
- Material quality
- Machine condition
- Operator training
- Production processes
- Defect categories
- Rework
- Scrap disposal
- Quality inspection

Similarly, a higher scrap cost does not always mean a product has a higher scrap rate. Product volume and unit production cost also affect the result.

These factors should be considered before making operational recommendations.

## ⚠️ Data Interpretation Notes

- The dataset is illustrative.
- Calculated results depend on the workbook's underlying data.
- Scrap cost is an estimate based on the unit production cost used in the workbook.
- Scrap cost should not automatically be added to cost of sales if that would double-count costs.
- Gross margin and margin after total production cost represent different calculations.
- A relationship between scrap and margin does not prove causation.
- Business recommendations should be based on evidence and appropriate operational context.
