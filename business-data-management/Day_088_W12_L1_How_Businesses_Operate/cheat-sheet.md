## Core Business Model

**Inputs → Processes → Outputs → Customers/Outcomes**

## Important Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Input | Resource used by a process |
| Process | Connected activities that transform inputs |
| Output | Result produced by a process |
| Customer | Person or organisation receiving value |
| Supplier | Provider of required resources |
| Revenue | Money earned from sales |
| Cost | Expense required to operate |
| Profit | Revenue minus cost |
| KPI | Key Performance Indicator |
| Operations | Activities involved in producing or delivering value |

## Basic Formulae

**Profit = Revenue − Cost**

**Average Order Value = Revenue / Orders**

**Cost per Order = Cost / Orders**

**On-Time Delivery Rate (%) = On-Time Deliveries / Total Deliveries × 100**

**Return Rate (%) = Returned Orders / Total Orders × 100**

**Profit Margin (%) = Profit / Revenue × 100**

## Business Functions

- Sales → orders and revenue
- Marketing → demand and customer communication
- Operations → production/service delivery
- Finance → money and financial control
- HR → workforce
- Procurement → purchasing
- Customer Service → support and feedback
- Technology → systems and data

## Process Thinking

Ask:

1. What is the input?
2. What activities happen?
3. What is the output?
4. Who receives the output?
5. What resources are required?
6. How is performance measured?
7. Where can delays or costs occur?

## KPI Categories

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Volume | Orders, units |
| Financial | Revenue, cost, profit |
| Customer | Satisfaction, returns |
| Time | Delivery time |
| Quality | Defect/return rate |
| Efficiency | Cost per order |

## Remember

- High revenue does not automatically mean high profit.
- High order volume can create capacity pressure.
- A KPI should be interpreted with its business context.
- Operational data describes performance; investigation is needed to establish causes.
