## 1. Introduction

A business is an organised system that uses resources and processes to create products or services for customers. Business operations describe the activities through which this value is created and delivered.

## 2. Business as a System

A simple business system can be understood as:

**Inputs → Processes → Outputs → Outcomes**

Inputs may include:

- Employees
- Money
- Materials
- Equipment
- Technology
- Information
- Time

Processes transform these inputs into useful outputs.

## 3. Customers and Value

Customers purchase a product or service because it provides value to them. Businesses therefore need to understand customer needs, deliver the expected quality, and maintain a sustainable operating model.

Customer feedback can be used to improve processes.

## 4. Suppliers

Suppliers provide materials, services, technology, or other resources required by a business.

Supplier performance can affect:

- Cost
- Quality
- Delivery time
- Inventory
- Production or service continuity

## 5. Major Business Functions

### Sales

Sales identifies opportunities, communicates with customers, receives orders, and contributes directly to revenue.

### Marketing

Marketing supports demand generation, communication, customer understanding, and market positioning.

### Operations

Operations performs the core activities needed to produce or deliver the business offering.

### Finance

Finance tracks revenue, expenses, cash, budgets, and financial performance.

### Human Resources

HR manages recruitment, employee records, training, compensation, and workforce planning.

### Procurement

Procurement obtains required goods and services from suppliers.

### Customer Service

Customer service handles questions, complaints, requests, and feedback.

### Technology

Technology supports applications, databases, communication, automation, analytics, and reporting.

## 6. Business Processes

A business process is a sequence of connected activities performed to achieve a specific result.

Example:

**Customer Order → Order Verification → Inventory Check → Packing → Delivery → Payment → Customer Feedback**

A process can be measured using operational data.

## 7. Revenue, Cost, and Profit

Revenue is the money earned from selling products or services.

Cost is the expenditure required to operate the business.

A simple relationship is:

**Profit = Revenue − Cost**

A business may increase sales but still experience weak profit if costs increase faster than revenue.

## 8. Operational KPIs

Useful operational measures include:

- Orders
- Units sold
- Revenue
- Cost
- Profit
- Average Order Value
- Delivery Time
- On-Time Delivery Rate
- Return Rate
- Customer Satisfaction

## 9. Process Performance

Process data helps answer questions such as:

- Which process handles the most orders?
- Which process generates the most revenue?
- Where are costs high?
- Which process has longer delivery time?
- Which function contributes to the process?
- Are quality or service indicators changing?

## 10. Example

Suppose an order moves through sales, operations, delivery, and customer service.

If order volume increases but delivery time also increases, management may need to examine capacity, staffing, inventory, or delivery processes.

The data shows the operational pattern. Further investigation is required before deciding why the pattern occurred.

## 11. Important Points

- Businesses are interconnected systems.
- Departments depend on one another.
- Processes connect business activities.
- KPIs convert operational activity into measurable information.
- A single KPI rarely explains complete business performance.
- Correlation in operational data should not automatically be treated as causation.

## 12. Summary

Business operations explain how an organisation uses resources, performs processes, creates value, and serves customers. Business data provides a structured way to measure these activities and support management decisions.
