## 1. Introduction

Day 002 begins the **Economics** component of the Business Data Management (BDM) course.

The session introduces the basic idea of **Economics** and explains how economic activities are connected through the flow of goods, services, factors of production, income, and expenditure.

Understanding economic flow provides an important foundation for analysing business and economic data.

---

## 2. What is Economics?

Economics is concerned with how economic activities take place and how different participants in an economy interact.

Economic activities involve the production, distribution, exchange, and consumption of goods and services.

The study of economics helps us understand how resources, goods, services, income, and expenditure move through an economy.

---

## 3. Economic Activities

Economic activities are activities related to the production, exchange, and consumption of goods and services.

Examples include:

- Producing goods
- Providing services
- Working for income
- Purchasing products
- Consuming services
- Investing
- Saving
- Paying taxes

These activities are interconnected rather than occurring independently.

---

## 4. Economic Agents

An economy consists of different participants, often referred to as **economic agents**.

Important participants include:

### Households

Households:

- Provide factors of production
- Earn income
- Purchase goods and services
- Spend money on consumption

### Firms

Firms:

- Produce goods and services
- Use factors of production
- Pay income to factor owners
- Sell goods and services

### Government

Government participates in economic activity through:

- Tax collection
- Government expenditure
- Public services
- Economic policies

---

## 5. Factors of Production

Firms require resources to produce goods and services.

Important factors of production include:

- Land
- Labour
- Capital
- Entrepreneurship

Households can provide factors of production to firms.

In return, households receive income.

---

## 6. Households and Firms

The relationship between households and firms is an important part of economic flow.

### Households → Firms

Households provide factors of production to firms.

Examples include:

- Labour
- Other productive resources

### Firms → Households

Firms provide income to households in return for the factors of production.

Examples include:

- Wages
- Other forms of factor income

---

## 7. Goods and Services Flow

Firms produce goods and services.

These goods and services can be purchased and consumed by households.

Therefore:

**Firms → Goods & Services → Households**

Households make expenditure in return:

**Households → Expenditure → Firms**

This creates a continuous interaction between households and firms.

---

## 8. Money Flow

Money moves in the opposite direction to many real economic activities.

For example:

**Households → Consumption Expenditure → Firms**

while:

**Firms → Income → Households**

Money therefore circulates through economic activities.

---

## 9. Real Flow

The movement of physical goods, services, and factors of production can be viewed as a **real flow**.

Examples include:

- Labour supplied by households
- Goods produced by firms
- Services provided by businesses

Real flow represents the movement of actual economic resources, goods, and services.

---

## 10. Money Flow

The movement of payments, income, and expenditure can be viewed as **money flow**.

Examples include:

- Wages paid to workers
- Household consumption expenditure
- Payments for goods and services
- Government expenditure

Money flow represents the monetary side of economic transactions.

---

## 11. Circular Economic Flow

Economic activity can be understood as a circular flow.

A simplified representation is:

**Households → Factors of Production → Firms**

**Firms → Income → Households**

**Firms → Goods & Services → Households**

**Households → Consumption Expenditure → Firms**

These interactions create a continuous circular flow of economic activity.

---

## 12. Simple Example

Consider a person working for a company.

### Step 1 — Labour

The person provides labour to the company.

### Step 2 — Production

The company uses labour and other resources to produce goods or services.

### Step 3 — Income

The company pays wages to the worker.

### Step 4 — Consumption

The worker uses income to purchase goods and services.

### Step 5 — Expenditure

The worker's spending becomes revenue for businesses.

This demonstrates how production, income, consumption, and expenditure are interconnected.

---

## 13. Economic Flow and Business Data

Economic activities generate data.

For example:

### Household Data

- Income
- Consumption
- Expenditure
- Savings

### Firm Data

- Production
- Sales
- Revenue
- Costs
- Employment

### Government Data

- Taxes
- Government expenditure
- Public spending

These data points can later be analysed to understand economic and business activities.

---

## 14. Business Data Perspective

The economic flow concept is useful for Business Data Management because businesses generate large amounts of data through their economic activities.

For example:

**Production → Production Data**

**Sales → Sales Data**

**Income → Income Data**

**Purchases → Purchase Data**

**Consumption → Consumption Data**

**Employment → Employment Data**

Understanding the economic activity behind the data helps in interpreting the data correctly.

---

## 15. Relationship Between Real Flow and Money Flow

Real flow and money flow are connected.

When a firm provides goods or services to a household, the household makes a corresponding payment.

Similarly, when a household provides labour to a firm, the firm provides income in return.

Therefore, economic transactions generally involve an exchange between real economic activity and monetary payments.

---

## 16. Simplified Economic Flow

The basic relationship can be remembered as:

**Households → Factors of Production → Firms**

**Firms → Income → Households**

**Firms → Goods & Services → Households**

**Households → Expenditure → Firms**

The flow continues as economic activities take place.

---

## 17. Why Economic Flow Matters

Understanding economic flow helps us understand:

- How households and firms interact
- How goods and services move
- How income is generated
- How expenditure takes place
- How production and consumption are connected
- How economic activities generate data

This provides a foundation for later business data analysis.

---

## 18. Important Terms

### Economics

Study of economic activities and interactions involving resources, production, consumption, and exchange.

### Economic Agent

A participant involved in economic activity.

### Household

An economic unit that can provide factors of production, earn income, and consume goods and services.

### Firm

An organisation that produces goods or services using resources and sells its output.

### Factor of Production

A resource used in the production of goods and services.

### Income

Money or earnings received by economic participants.

### Expenditure

Money spent to purchase goods and services.

### Real Flow

Flow of goods, services, and factors of production.

### Money Flow

Flow of income, payments, and expenditure.

---

## 19. Key Takeaways

- Economics studies economic activities and interactions.
- Households and firms are important economic participants.
- Households can provide factors of production to firms.
- Firms use factors of production to produce goods and services.
- Firms provide income to households in return for productive resources.
- Households purchase goods and services from firms.
- Household expenditure provides revenue to firms.
- Real flow represents goods, services, and factors of production.
- Money flow represents payments, income, and expenditure.
- Economic activities generate useful business and economic data.
