## 🔄 Economic Flow

Economic activities involve continuous interactions between different participants.

A simplified representation is:

**Households ↔ Firms**

Households provide factors of production to firms and receive income in return.

Firms produce goods and services and sell them to households in exchange for expenditure.

This creates a continuous flow within the economy.

---

## 🏠 Major Economic Participants

### Households

Households participate in the economy by:

- Providing factors of production
- Earning income
- Purchasing goods and services
- Spending money on consumption

### Firms

Firms participate by:

- Producing goods and services
- Hiring factors of production
- Paying income to households
- Selling goods and services

### Government

The government also participates in economic activity through activities such as:

- Collecting taxes
- Providing public services
- Making expenditures
- Participating in economic policy

---

## 💰 Types of Economic Flows

### Real Flow

The movement of:

- Goods
- Services
- Factors of production

### Money Flow

The movement of:

- Income
- Payments
- Expenditure

These flows are connected to each other.

---

## 📊 Economic Flow Example

A simplified example:

**Household → Labour → Firm**

**Firm → Wages → Household**

**Firm → Goods & Services → Household**

**Household → Consumption Expenditure → Firm**

This shows how production, income, consumption, and expenditure are connected.

---

## 💡 Why Economic Flow Matters in BDM

Understanding economic flow helps in understanding business and economic data.

Business data can represent activities such as:

- Sales
- Purchases
- Income
- Expenditure
- Production
- Employment
- Consumption

Understanding the economic relationships behind these activities makes it easier to interpret business data.

---

## 🛠️ Data Perspective

Economic activities generate measurable data.

For example:

- Number of products sold
- Revenue
- Wages
- Household expenditure
- Production quantity
- Government expenditure
- Tax collection

Such data can later be organised and analysed using tools such as spreadsheets.
