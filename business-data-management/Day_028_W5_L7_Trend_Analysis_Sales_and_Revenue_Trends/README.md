## 📈 Monthly Trend Analysis

Monthly trend analysis groups observations by month.

Orders can be compared between months.

Units can be compared between months.

Revenue can be compared between months.

The months should remain in chronological order.

A line chart is suitable for monthly trends.

The direction of the line helps show movement.

A rising line indicates increasing values.

A falling line indicates decreasing values.

A flat line indicates relatively stable values.

## 💰 Revenue Trend

Revenue is an important business performance measure.

Monthly revenue can show the movement of sales value.

The first month provides the starting point.

The last month provides the ending point.

The difference between the two helps describe overall movement.

Month-over-month growth provides a more detailed comparison.

Revenue should not be interpreted without checking units.

## 📦 Sales Volume Trend

Units represent sales volume.

Unit trends help explain demand movement.

A rise in units can indicate higher sales volume.

A decline in units can indicate lower sales volume.

Units and revenue may move differently.

This difference can indicate changes in product mix or value.

Therefore both measures should be reviewed.

## 📅 Weekday Analysis

Weekday analysis compares sales across days.

Monday can be compared with Tuesday.

Tuesday can be compared with Wednesday.

Wednesday can be compared with Thursday.

Thursday can be compared with Friday.

Friday can be compared with Saturday.

The comparison helps identify recurring patterns in the sample.

The result is descriptive rather than causal.

## 🧮 Growth Calculation

Growth percentage compares two periods.

The basic formula is:

(Current Value − Previous Value) / Previous Value × 100

A positive value represents growth.

A negative value represents decline.

The first month does not have a previous month.

Therefore its growth value is normally blank.

## 🧠 Business Interpretation

Trend analysis should focus on direction.

A single high value does not automatically represent a long-term trend.

Multiple periods provide stronger evidence.

Revenue and units should be compared together.

Product performance can explain overall movement.

Channel performance can add business context.

Regional performance can add geographic context.

Weekday patterns can support operational decisions.
